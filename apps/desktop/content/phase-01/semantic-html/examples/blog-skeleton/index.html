Why analogies stick

By Jane Doe · March 12, 2026

I used to think analogies were a soft skill — a nice-to-have for explaining things to friends at parties. Then I started teaching code, and I realized analogies are the entire game.

When I explain closures with words alone, students nod. When I say "closures are a backpack the function carries with it," they laugh, and a week later they still remember.

The mechanism

An analogy gives an abstract idea a physical handle. Once you have something to grab, you can move it around in your head. That's all "understanding" really is.

Where they go wrong

An analogy can also overpromise. If you tell a beginner "JavaScript objects are like JavaScript classes," you've told them a lie they'll have to unlearn. Pick the analogy carefully — and tell them where it leaks.
